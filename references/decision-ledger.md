# Decision Ledger

`SKILL.md` and `references/detection-guidance.md` are prohibitions: what a tell looks like, and
what only looks like one. Neither can tell you what *this* author sounds like when they are
writing well. That comes from lines they have accepted, and it accumulates only if somebody
writes it down.

Keep the ledger with the project, not with this skill. The skill stays domain-agnostic; house
terminology, register decisions, and banked rulings belong in an overlay that sits on top of it
and wins conflicts, the same way a voice sample outranks §14.

## Record a ruling when the user accepts or rejects a specific line

Five fields, one entry:

- **Original.** The wording as it stood.
- **Accepted.** The wording that won. For a rejection with no replacement, say what was cut.
- **Why it won.** One sentence. This is the field the ledger exists for.
- **Surface and register.** A PR description, a forum reply, and a conference abstract can rule
  differently on the same sentence without either ruling being wrong.
- **Exception.** Where the ruling does not apply, when you already know.

## Classify the reason, because it decides how far the ruling travels

Six classes, in descending order of reach:

| Class | Reaches |
|---|---|
| Truth | Everything. A ruling about accuracy is not a style preference and does not get overridden by voice. |
| Clarity | Most surfaces, unless a register deliberately trades clarity for compression. |
| Continuity | The sequence it belongs to: a doc set, a course, a campaign. |
| Register | The surface named in the entry, and nothing else. |
| Rhythm | The passage. Rhythm rulings generalize badly; a cadence that fixes one paragraph flattens the next. |
| Taste | This line. Record it, apply it when the same line comes back, and do not extrapolate. |

The mistake this table exists to prevent is promoting a taste ruling to a house rule. One reader
disliking one *however* is not evidence that *however* is banned, and a ledger that treats it as
one will strip a legitimate connective from every future draft. When you cannot tell which class
a ruling belongs to, ask, or file it as taste: under-generalizing costs one repeated correction,
while over-generalizing quietly removes something the author uses on purpose.

## Read the ledger before rewriting, not after

Banked rulings are worth more than the pattern list when the two disagree, because they are
evidence from this author about this kind of text, and the patterns are evidence about writing in
general. Apply them in the draft. Discovering after the final rewrite that a banked line was
overwritten costs the user the same correction twice, which is the specific failure that makes an
editor feel like it is not listening.

A ledger that only holds rejections becomes another ban list, and the reason for keeping one is
that positive examples teach voice and prohibitions do not. When a line survives review because
it is good rather than merely acceptable, that is an entry too.
